# Appendix I — Starting Hands Reference

---

## Why This Appendix Exists

Preflop hand selection is the first decision in every hand and the foundation of all subsequent strategy. Every hand in this reference has distinct equity characteristics, positional requirements, and postflop playability profiles. Understanding not just which hands are strong but *why* they are strong — and where they fail — is what separates mechanical play from strategic play.

This appendix is organized by hand category. Each entry covers:

- **Strength**: what makes the hand valuable
- **Weakness**: where the hand loses equity or becomes difficult to play
- **Guidelines**: 1–3 sentences on how to deploy it correctly

Suit notation: ♠ ♥ ♦ ♣
**s** = suited, **o** = offsuit

---

## 1. Premium Pairs

---

### A♠ A♥ — Pocket Aces (Rockets)

**Strength:** The best starting hand in No-Limit Hold'em. Approximately 85% equity against any single random hand. Dominates all other pairs and all unpaired hands. Can be played for stacks preflop against any reasonable opponent range.

**Weakness:** Loses value in multiway pots where combined opponent equity overtakes the pair. Vulnerable to sets on wet boards — an opponent who flops a set of twos against aces is a large favorite. Aces that are not bet aggressively allow too many hands to see flops cheaply and crack them.

**Guidelines:** Raise or re-raise from any position; never limp. Build the pot preflop to reduce the number of opponents and increase the chance of winning without showdown. Bet for value on most flops and be prepared to stack off — folding aces postflop is correct in rare, specific situations (obvious set on a dry board with heavy action) but should never become a default.

---

### K♠ K♥ — Pocket Kings (Cowboys)

**Strength:** Second-best starting hand. Approximately 82% equity against a random hand. Dominates all hands except aces, and even against aces retains approximately 18% equity. Correct to play for stacks preflop in most situations.

**Weakness:** The only hand that clearly dominates kings preflop is aces (approximately 18% equity). On ace-high flops, the value of kings drops sharply — an opponent who has an ace in their range now has top pair or better. Kings require reads on opponents to navigate ace-high boards correctly.

**Guidelines:** 4-bet for value against 3-bets in most situations; folding kings preflop is almost never correct except in very specific tournament ICM spots. On ace-high flops, slow down against heavy action — bet once for value and evaluate, rather than committing for stacks automatically.

---

### Q♠ Q♥ — Pocket Queens (Ladies)

**Strength:** Third-best starting hand. Strong equity against all unpaired hands and most pairs. Correct to play for stacks preflop against wide opponent ranges.

**Weakness:** Dominated by both aces and kings preflop. On ace-high or king-high flops, top pair defeats queens with no improvement. Queens play best in heads-up pots on low-to-medium boards where their equity advantage is largest.

**Guidelines:** 3-bet and 4-bet for value from all positions. On ace-high or king-high flops against heavy resistance, reduce commitment — queens become a bluff catcher rather than a stacking hand. SPR awareness is critical: low SPR favors stacking off, high SPR favors caution.

---

### J♠ J♥ — Pocket Jacks (Hooks)

**Strength:** Fourth-best starting hand. Dominates all pairs below tens and all unpaired hands without a jack, king, queen, or ace. Strong preflop equity in a vacuum.

**Weakness:** Dominated by AA, KK, QQ preflop. Any flop containing an ace, king, or queen — which occurs roughly 57% of the time with three such cards in the deck — puts jacks in a difficult spot against opponents who hold those cards. Jacks are frequently described as the most misplayed hand in poker because players over-commit postflop on boards that hit opponent ranges.

**Guidelines:** Open raise and 3-bet from most positions, but exercise more caution about 4-bet/calling off stacks than with QQ and above. On A/K/Q-high flops, treat as a bluff catcher by default unless the board is otherwise favorable. In 3-bet pots, check-call is often preferable to check-raise on broadway-heavy boards.

---

### T♠ T♥ — Pocket Tens

**Strength:** A strong pair that dominates all pairs below tens and beats all unpaired hands cleanly. Excellent equity in heads-up pots. Retains value as an overpair on low boards.

**Weakness:** Dominated by four over-pairs (JJ, QQ, KK, AA). Any flop containing a jack or higher — which occurs approximately 70% of the time with four such card ranks in the deck — turns tens into a middle pair or worse. Tens are a premium hand preflop that frequently becomes a medium-strength hand postflop.

**Guidelines:** Open raise from all positions and 3-bet in late position and against wide ranges. Postflop, play as a strong hand only on low boards (9-high or below); treat cautiously on any board with a jack, queen, king, or ace against continued aggression. Set-mining value is secondary to their equity as an overpair.

## 2. Medium Pairs

---

### 9♠ 9♥ — Pocket Nines

**Strength:** A solid pair that functions as both an overpair to low boards and a set-mining hand. Approximately 72% equity heads-up against two random overcards.

**Weakness:** Five overcards (T, J, Q, K, A) outdraw nines on flops. On boards with any of these cards, nines become a medium pair with limited one-pair value.

**Guidelines:** Open raise from all positions. In 3-bet pots, play cautiously postflop unless the board is below nine-high. As stacks get shallower (below 30–40 BB), nines become stronger shove/call hands preflop due to reduced postflop complexity.

---

### 8♠ 8♥ — Pocket Eights

**Strength:** A pair with decent preflop equity and strong set-mining upside. A flopped set of eights is well-disguised and extremely valuable.

**Weakness:** Becomes a medium pair on the majority of flops. Difficult to play for large pots without improvement. Vulnerable to both overcards and board pairs.

**Guidelines:** Open raise from all positions; call 3-bets with deep stacks (implied odds for sets). Without improvement, keep pots small and be willing to fold to heavy postflop action — do not overcommit with just a pair of eights against a board that connects with opponent ranges.

---

### 7♠ 7♥ — Pocket Sevens

**Strength:** Primarily a set-mining hand. Heads-up preflop equity is reasonable but the main value is flopping a set (~12% probability) and extracting large pots from opponents with top pair and overpairs.

**Weakness:** As a pure pair, sevens have little one-pair value on most boards. Without hitting a set, the hand plays defensively.

**Guidelines:** Open from all positions; call raises in position with sufficient implied odds for set-mining (generally 15:1 or better ratio of stack to call amount). If no set is flopped, play small-pot poker and fold to significant pressure.

---

### 6♠ 6♥ — Pocket Sixes

**Strength:** Same set-mining profile as sevens. When sets are flopped, the hand is extremely disguised on boards that appear safe for opponent top-pair hands.

**Weakness:** Minimal one-pair value. Even a set of sixes can be vulnerable on connected boards where opponents have straights or flush draws.

**Guidelines:** Play primarily for set value. Open from late position and call raises with correct implied odds. If a set is not flopped, fold to most postflop aggression unless the pot is small and the opponent appears weak.

---

### 5♠ 5♥ through 2♠ 2♥ — Small Pairs

**Strength:** Pure set-mining hands. Flopped sets are powerful and disguised. Opponent top-pair and two-pair hands will pay off large bets without suspecting they are dominated.

**Weakness:** Almost no playable value without a set. Two pair with a small pocket pair is also vulnerable on many boards. The set itself can occasionally lose to straights and flushes.

**Guidelines:** Open from late position only in unopened pots. Call raises only with deep stacks and clear implied odds (generally 20:1 or better). Fold on any flop without a set unless the pot is tiny and a free or cheap card is available. Never overcommit with just a pair.

## 3. Strong Broadway Hands

---

### A♠ K♠ — Ace-King Suited (Big Slick Suited)

**Strength:** The strongest non-pair starting hand. Approximately 47% equity against a random pair (near coin flip vs most pairs, slight favorite vs pairs below queens). Nut flush draw and nut straight draws when suited. Blocks the top of opponent ranges (holds an ace and a king, reducing the likelihood opponents have AA or KK).

**Weakness:** Does not flop a pair or better approximately 67% of the time. Without improvement, AKs is air on most boards. Overvaluing unimproved AK postflop — calling large bets or barreling on dry boards with no pair — is one of the most common mistakes.

**Guidelines:** 3-bet and 4-bet for value from all positions; a standard play against a wide range of opponents. On flops where it misses, proceed cautiously unless a draw is present — AK with a flush draw or straight draw retains strong equity and should be played aggressively. When the flop brings a top pair (ace or king), bet for value on most textures.

---

### A♠ K♦ — Ace-King Offsuit (Big Slick)

**Strength:** Same raw equity profile as AKs against pairs and unpaired hands. Dominates all other ace-x and king-x holdings.

**Weakness:** No flush draw value. On wet boards, AKo has less equity than AKs because it cannot pick up a nut flush draw. Slightly more vulnerable in multiway pots.

**Guidelines:** Play identically to AKs preflop — 3-bet and 4-bet for value. Postflop, be slightly more conservative on very wet boards where AKs would have the flush draw as additional equity. On dry boards, the difference from AKs is negligible.

---

### A♠ Q♠ — Ace-Queen Suited

**Strength:** Strong broadway hand. Dominates AJ, AT, KQ, and all weaker ace-x holdings. Flush draw and straight draw potential. Near coin flip equity versus pairs JJ and below.

**Weakness:** Dominated by AK and AA. On ace-high boards in 3-bet pots against aggressive opponents, AQ may be behind a larger ace. In 4-bet pots, AQ is frequently dominated.

**Guidelines:** Open raise and 3-bet from all positions. In 4-bet spots, prefer a call to a shove unless the opponent's 4-bet range is wide. On ace-high boards, bet for value but be cautious in large pots facing heavy resistance — you may be outkicked.

---

### A♠ Q♦ — Ace-Queen Offsuit

**Strength:** Strong preflop hand. Dominates the same hands as AQs with the same raw equity.

**Weakness:** No flush draw equity. More vulnerable in multiway pots and on wet boards. In 3-bet pots, AQo out of position is particularly difficult to play — the hand is too strong to fold preflop but awkward postflop on many textures.

**Guidelines:** Open from all positions; 3-bet from late position against late-position opens. Avoid 3-betting AQo out of position against early position opens — calling and playing postflop is often better. In 4-bet situations, folding is frequently correct against tight 4-bet ranges.

---

### K♠ Q♠ — King-Queen Suited

**Strength:** Strong broadway hand with flush draw and straight draw potential. Dominates KJ, KT, QJ, and weaker holdings. Strong heads-up equity against single opponents.

**Weakness:** Dominated by AK, AQ, and all pairs above queens. On ace-high boards, KQ loses its pair value entirely unless the board is specifically K or Q high. In multiway pots, the hand loses equity quickly.

**Guidelines:** Open raise and 3-bet from late position. On king-high flops, top pair is valuable and should be bet for value. On ace-high flops, proceed cautiously — KQ is a second-best hand on most such boards. Avoid calling 4-bets with KQs except against very wide 4-bet ranges.

---

### K♠ Q♦ — King-Queen Offsuit

**Strength:** Similar raw equity to KQs without the flush draw. Strong heads-up equity in position.

**Weakness:** No flush draw. Dominated by most of the hands that 3-bet from early position. Out of position, KQo is difficult to play profitably in 3-bet pots.

**Guidelines:** Open from all positions. 3-bet only from the button and cutoff against late-position opens. Avoid calling 3-bets out of position — the hand plays better in position where the lack of a flush draw is less punishing.

---

## 4. Suited Aces

---

### A♠ J♠ — Ace-Jack Suited

**Strength:** Dominated by only three hands (AA, AK, AQ). Strong flush draw and straight draw potential. Top pair ace on ace-high boards is strong with a moderate kicker.

**Weakness:** Reverse implied odds against AK and AQ on ace-high boards — top pair with a jack kicker can lose significant chips to a better kicker. In 4-bet pots, AJs is frequently dominated.

**Guidelines:** Open and 3-bet from late position. On ace-high boards, bet for value but be wary of heavy resistance — the kicker is vulnerable. On jack-high boards, top pair with top kicker is a strong hand worth value betting multiple streets.

---

### A♠ T♠ — Ace-Ten Suited

**Strength:** The Broadway wheel component — A-T completes the bottom of the broadway straight (A-T-J-Q-K). Flush draw, straight draw potential. Reasonable equity in heads-up pots.

**Weakness:** Dominated by AJ, AQ, AK, and all pairs above tens. Reverse implied odds against better aces on ace-high boards. Mediocre kicker strength.

**Guidelines:** Open from all positions; 3-bet from late position against wide ranges. On ace-high boards, play medium-pot poker — bet once for value and avoid building large pots without improvement. Particularly strong on J-Q-K or T-J-Q boards where straight potential materializes.

---

### A♠ 5♠ through A♠ 2♠ — Suited Aces (Wheel Aces)

**Strength:** The primary value is the **nut flush draw** — holding the ace of a suit guarantees the highest possible flush when the draw completes. Secondary value: the wheel straight (A-2-3-4-5) is available, which is well-disguised. **Blocker value** — holding an ace reduces the probability opponents have AA or AK.

**Weakness:** Minimal pair value — a paired ace with a 2–5 kicker is weak and loses to any better kicker. The low card is often a liability in any situation other than completing the flush or straight.

**Guidelines:** Play primarily for the nut flush draw equity — the hand is stronger on suit-heavy boards. Open from late position and call raises in position with deep stacks. Do not overvalue a paired ace — the kicker is the weakest possible. In tournament contexts, the blocker value (holding an ace) has additional usefulness in 3-bet bluff spots.

---

## 5. Suited Connectors

---

### J♠ T♠ — Jack-Ten Suited

**Strength:** The strongest suited connector. Can make straights in both directions (7-8-9-J-T and T-J-Q-K-A). Nut straight draws occur frequently. Flush draw equity is strong. Can flop top pair with reasonable kicker on jack-high or ten-high boards.

**Weakness:** Vulnerable to domination on broadway boards where opponents have A-J, A-T, K-J, K-T. Does not flop well on low boards (2-5-8 type) where the hand has no equity.

**Guidelines:** Open from all positions and call raises in position for both flush and straight draw potential. On J-T-x or T-J-x boards, top pair/top pair or two pair is strong — bet multiple streets for value. On boards that miss entirely, play small pot or fold to aggression.

---

### T♠ 9♠ — Ten-Nine Suited

**Strength:** Excellent straight draw potential — connects to 6-7-8, 7-8-J, 8-J-Q among others. Strong flush draw. Well-disguised on medium boards where opponents hold overpairs and top pair.

**Weakness:** No natural pair equity on most broadway boards. Dominated on T-high boards by ATs, KTs. Lower flush draws (nine-high flush) can lose to higher flushes on suit-heavy boards.

**Guidelines:** Play for implied odds — the hand makes strong disguised holdings that extract value from top-pair hands that will not fold. Open from late position; call raises in position with sufficient stack depth. On boards where draws do not develop, give up against aggression.

---

### 9♠ 8♠ — Nine-Eight Suited

**Strength:** Strong straight draw potential. Medium flush draw. Disguised on boards that appear safe for opponents (a straight on 7-6-5 or 6-7-T is invisible to a player holding a top pair king or queen).

**Weakness:** Lower flush draw than higher suited connectors — nine-high flush loses to any higher flush. On boards where it flops a pair only, the hand has weak value.

**Guidelines:** Best played in position and in multiway pots where implied odds are highest. Open from late position; call small raises with deep stacks. Fold in early position to 3-bets unless the implied odds are exceptional.

---

### 8♠ 7♠ — Eight-Seven Suited

**Strength:** Strong straight and flush draw potential. Extremely well-disguised on low boards. Opponents holding overpairs and top pair on 5-6-9 or 6-7-T boards will pay off straights liberally.

**Weakness:** Low flush draw (seven-high flush loses to many better flushes on suit-heavy boards). Minimal high-card value. Very position-dependent.

**Guidelines:** Play in late position and on the button primarily. Requires deep stacks to justify the implied odds investment. Fold in early position and to 3-bets in marginal spots. The hand's value is almost entirely in flopping a strong draw or disguised made hand.

---

### 7♠ 6♠ — Seven-Six Suited

**Strength:** Low suited connector with straight potential in both directions. Well-disguised on low boards. Strong implied odds when straights complete.

**Weakness:** Low flush draw susceptible to higher flushes. Minimal value on high boards. Position-dependent.

**Guidelines:** Play only from late position in unopened or raised pots with deep stacks. The hand needs to flop a draw or hit a disguised made hand to have value — without that, fold to any significant postflop pressure.

---

### K♠ Q♠ / Q♠ J♠ — Suited Broadway Connectors

*(KQs is covered in Section 3. QJs follows the same profile.)*

**Q♠ J♠ Strength:** Strong broadway connector. Flush draw, straight draw, top pair potential on Q-high and J-high boards. Dominates QT, JT, and weaker holdings.

**Q♠ J♠ Weakness:** Dominated by AQ, AJ, KQ, KJ on broadway boards. Flush draws are not the nut flush.

**Guidelines:** Open from all positions; 3-bet from late position. On Q-high or J-high boards, bet two streets for value. On ace-king-high boards, proceed with caution — top pair with these holdings is frequently second best.

---

## 6. Offsuit Broadway Hands

---

### A♠ J♦ — Ace-Jack Offsuit

**Strength:** Dominates all weaker aces and jacks. Strong top pair potential on ace-high and jack-high boards.

**Weakness:** Dominated by AK and AQ with reverse implied odds on ace-high boards. No flush draw. Out of position in 3-bet pots, the hand is very difficult to play.

**Guidelines:** Open from all positions. 3-bet only from the button and cutoff. Avoid 3-betting out of position — AJo is a calling hand from the blinds against late-position opens, not a 3-betting hand. On ace-high boards, one street of value then caution against heavy resistance.

---

### K♠ J♦ — King-Jack Offsuit

**Strength:** Top pair potential on king-high and jack-high boards. Dominates KT, QJ, JT.

**Weakness:** Dominated by AK, AJ, KQ. On ace-high boards, entirely without value. No flush draw. Reverse implied odds against better kickers on king-high boards.

**Guidelines:** Open from late position only. Avoid calling 3-bets without strong position and deep stacks. On king-high boards, bet once for value and evaluate — the kicker vulnerability is real against 3-betting ranges.

---

### Q♠ J♦ — Queen-Jack Offsuit

**Strength:** Connected broadway hand. Top pair on Q-high and J-high boards. Straight potential.

**Weakness:** Dominated by many 3-betting hands. No flush draw. Difficult to continue against aggression without a strong board texture.

**Guidelines:** Open from late position. Fold to 3-bets without strong position and read. On Q-high or J-high boards in heads-up pots, play for medium-sized value — the hand is rarely a stacking hand but generates value in small and medium pots.

---

### K♠ T♦ — King-Ten Offsuit

**Strength:** Decent broadway coverage. Top pair on K-high and T-high boards. Backdoor straight potential.

**Weakness:** Dominated by AK, KQ, KJ, AT. On ace-high boards, entirely valueless. Weak kicker on king-high boards.

**Guidelines:** Open from late position only. Do not call 3-bets. On king-high boards, one street of thin value; fold to heavy resistance. The hand plays better in unopened pots where its top-pair potential is not immediately dominated.

---

## 7. Speculative and Borderline Hands

---

### K♠ 9♠ — King-Nine Suited

**Strength:** Flush draw potential (king-high flush). Top pair possible on king-high boards. A reasonable speculative hand in position.

**Weakness:** Weak kicker — K9 top pair loses to K-T, K-J, K-Q. Not a strong hand in 3-bet pots. Flush draw is king-high, not the nut flush.

**Guidelines:** Open from the cutoff and button. Fold to 3-bets. On king-high boards, proceed with caution — the kicker is a liability against any reasonable opponent range.

---

### J♠ 9♠ — Jack-Nine Suited

**Strength:** Good straight draw potential. Flush draw. Can flop two pair or a straight draw on medium boards (T-8-x, 8-7-x). Well-disguised.

**Weakness:** Not a strong high-card hand. Dominated on J-high boards by better jacks. Low flush draw susceptible to higher flushes.

**Guidelines:** Play in late position for implied odds. Requires a specific flop — a strong draw or two pair — to continue. Fold to 3-bets without strong position.

---

### T♠ 8♠ — Ten-Eight Suited

**Strength:** One-gapper suited connector. Straight draws are available on 7-9-x and 9-J-x boards. Flush draw. Reasonable implied odds hand.

**Weakness:** Lower connectivity than T9s or 98s. More vulnerable to domination. Flush draw is ten-high — not the nut flush.

**Guidelines:** Play from late position only. The hand needs to hit a specific strong draw to justify continued investment. Fold to significant preflop pressure.

---

### 9♠ 7♠ — Nine-Seven Suited

**Strength:** One-gapper suited connector with disguised straight potential. On 8-6-x or 6-8-T boards, the straight is invisible.

**Weakness:** Gap in the connector reduces straight draw frequency. Low flush draw. Minimal high-card value.

**Guidelines:** Speculative hand for late position only. Requires deep stacks and good position. Fold to any meaningful 3-bet or preflop aggression.

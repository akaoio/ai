# 06 — Board Texture

The board (the community cards) does not exist in isolation. Its specific combination of cards interacts with every possible hand range. Understanding board texture — what the board means for each player's range — is critical for making correct bets, calls, and folds.

---

## Wet vs Dry Boards

**Dry boards** have few possible draws. Characteristics: mixed suits (rainbow), disconnected ranks, low card combinations.

Example: K♠ 7♦ 2♣ — no flush draw possible, only a gutshot straight draw exists (A-3, 3-4 are very unlikely holdings), and the board is safe for a preflop raiser's strong range.

**Wet boards** have many possible draws. Characteristics: two or more cards of the same suit, connected ranks, high-card boards that interact with broadway holdings.

Example: J♥ 9♥ 8♦ — flush draws, open-ended straight draws, combo draws. Many hands have strong equity even without a made hand.

The wetness of a board determines how much protection betting for value provides, how much draw-heavy ranges benefit from seeing the next card cheaply, and how much the range of possible strong hands expands.

---

## Paired Boards

When the board contains two cards of the same rank (e.g., Q♠ Q♦ 7♣), the dynamics shift significantly. Full houses and four-of-a-kind become possible. Top pair is devalued because many opponents who call have trips, a full house, or a strong queen. Bluffing becomes more complicated because the board interacts with fewer hands, and a strong bet on a paired board is more credible.

---

## Broadway Boards

Boards containing high cards (T, J, Q, K, A) interact heavily with preflop raising ranges, which contain many broadway hands (A-K, A-Q, K-Q, K-J, etc.). A board of A-K-Q is dramatically favorable for the preflop raiser but dangerous for callers holding speculative hands. Conversely, a board of A-K-Q benefits both players if both had strong ranges preflop.

---

## Low Connected Boards

Boards like 6-5-4 or 7-6-5 interact very poorly with preflop raising ranges (which rarely contain 4-5, 5-6, 7-8 type hands) but are excellent for callers who defend with suited connectors and small pairs. This is why c-betting into a caller on low connected boards is less effective than on high, dry boards.

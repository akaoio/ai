# 03 — Betting Mechanics

---

## Blinds

Before any cards are dealt, two players post **forced bets** to seed the pot and create incentive to play:

- **Small blind (SB)**: Posted by the player immediately to the left of the dealer button. Typically half the big blind, though this can vary.
- **Big blind (BB)**: Posted by the player two seats to the left of the button. This is the standard minimum bet for the table and defines the denomination of the game. A "$1/$2 game" means $1 small blind and $2 big blind.

The big blind is the base unit of the game. All stack sizes, bets, and winrates are expressed relative to it. A player with 200 chips in a $1/$2 game has a 100 BB stack.

In some formats, an **ante** is also posted — a smaller forced bet from every player, not just the two blinds. Antes increase the starting pot size, accelerate play, and reward aggressive stealing of uncontested pots.

---

## Preflop Betting Order

Preflop, the player to the left of the big blind acts first (this position is called Under the Gun, or UTG). Action proceeds clockwise. The small blind acts second-to-last and the big blind acts last preflop, giving the big blind the unique ability to **close the action** or exercise a **squeeze** by re-raising after multiple players have called.

---

## Postflop Betting Order

On the flop, turn, and river, the small blind acts first if still in the hand, followed by the big blind, and so on clockwise to the button who acts last. This order is fixed throughout all postflop streets.

---

## Actions Available to a Player

At any point when it is a player's turn to act, they may take one of the following actions:

**Fold**
Discard the hand and relinquish any claim to the pot. A folded player cannot win the hand. All chips already invested remain in the pot. Folding is never forced, but it is often correct.

**Check**
Pass the action to the next player without committing any chips. A player may only check when no bet has been made yet in the current round. If all players check, the round ends and the next card is dealt (or showdown occurs).

**Call**
Match the amount of the current bet or raise. This keeps the player in the hand without increasing the stakes.

**Bet**
Initiate wagering by placing chips into the pot. A bet can only be made when no bet has yet been placed in the current round. Once a bet is made, subsequent players must call, raise, or fold.

**Raise**
Increase the size of an existing bet. A raise must be at least as large as the previous bet or raise in the same round. Example: if Player A bets 10 and Player B raises to 30, the minimum re-raise by Player C would be to 50 (the raise increment was 20, so the next raise must be at least 20 more than the current bet of 30).

**All-in**
Commit all remaining chips to the pot. A player who cannot match a bet in full may go all-in for their remaining chips and is entitled to win from each opponent only up to the amount they put in. Going all-in protects a player from being forced out of a pot they cannot afford to call in full.

---

## Pot Types — Main Pot and Side Pots

When a player goes all-in for an amount less than the full bet, the pot is split:

- **Main pot**: Contains chips up to the all-in player's contribution from each remaining player. The all-in player is eligible to win this pot.
- **Side pot**: Contains chips above the all-in amount contributed by the remaining active players. The all-in player cannot win any side pot.

Multiple side pots can exist simultaneously if several players go all-in for different amounts. Each side pot is contested only by the players who contributed to it.

Example: Player A (50 chips), Player B (200 chips), Player C (200 chips). Player A goes all-in for 50. Player B calls 200, Player C calls 200.

- Main pot = 50 × 3 = 150 chips (all three eligible)
- Side pot = 150 × 2 = 300 chips (only B and C eligible)

---

## No-Limit Rules

In **No-Limit** Hold'em, any player may bet or raise any amount from the minimum up to their entire remaining stack at any time. This is the dominant format in modern poker. The possibility of a massive bet at any moment creates extreme pressure and decision complexity.

In contrast:

- **Limit Hold'em**: bets and raises are fixed in size; two bet sizes exist (small bet preflop/flop, big bet turn/river)
- **Pot-Limit Hold'em**: maximum bet is the current size of the pot

No-Limit is the standard for competitive and professional play.

---

## Betting Rounds and Cap

Each betting round continues until all active players have put in equal amounts or folded. There is no cap on the number of raises per round in No-Limit Hold'em (though heads-up play has historically allowed unlimited raises; table caps may apply in some live settings).

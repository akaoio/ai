# 02 — Hand Rankings

The following rankings determine which player wins at showdown. Rankings are fixed and do not vary by game variant.

---

## 1. Royal Flush

The absolute best hand in poker. Five cards of the same suit in sequence from ten to ace: A♠ K♠ Q♠ J♠ 10♠. Only one Royal Flush per suit exists in a deck. Two players cannot both hold a Royal Flush in the same hand. It is extremely rare.

## 2. Straight Flush

Five consecutive cards all of the same suit. Example: 8♥ 7♥ 6♥ 5♥ 4♥. The highest card in the sequence determines the rank of the straight flush. A Royal Flush is technically the highest possible straight flush.

## 3. Four of a Kind (Quads)

Four cards of the same rank, plus any fifth card (the kicker). Example: K♠ K♥ K♦ K♣ 9♠. If two players both hold four of a kind (only possible in a community card game), the higher rank wins. If the rank is the same, the kicker determines the winner.

## 4. Full House

Three cards of one rank combined with two cards of another rank. Example: Q♠ Q♥ Q♦ 7♣ 7♦. When comparing full houses, the three-of-a-kind rank takes priority. Queens full of sevens beats Jacks full of aces.

## 5. Flush

Five cards of the same suit, not in sequence. Example: A♣ J♣ 8♣ 5♣ 2♣. The highest card in the flush determines its rank. If the highest card ties, the second highest is compared, and so on down to the fifth card.

## 6. Straight

Five consecutive cards of mixed suits. Example: 9♠ 8♥ 7♦ 6♣ 5♠. Aces can play high (A-K-Q-J-10) or low (A-2-3-4-5, called a wheel). A-K-Q-J-10 is the highest straight; A-2-3-4-5 is the lowest. The highest card of the straight determines its rank.

## 7. Three of a Kind (Trips or a Set)

Three cards of the same rank plus two unrelated cards. Example: 7♠ 7♥ 7♦ K♣ 2♠. When the three of a kind is made using a pocket pair plus one community card, it is called a **set**. When it is made using one hole card and two community cards of matching rank, it is called **trips**. Sets are generally more disguised and more valuable in practice.

## 8. Two Pair

Two cards of one rank, two cards of another rank, and one unrelated kicker. Example: A♠ A♦ 8♥ 8♣ Q♦. The higher pair is compared first; if equal, the lower pair; if still equal, the kicker.

## 9. One Pair

Two cards of the same rank and three unrelated cards. Example: J♠ J♣ A♥ 7♦ 3♠. The rank of the pair determines strength; if equal, kickers are compared in descending order.

## 10. High Card

No combination formed. The hand is judged by its highest card, then second highest, and so on. Example: A♠ Q♣ 9♦ 6♥ 2♠ beats K♠ Q♣ 9♦ 6♥ 2♠ because the ace plays over the king.

---

## Kickers

A kicker is a card not involved in the primary combination that is used as a tiebreaker. Example: both players hold one pair of aces, but Player A has A-A-K-7-2 and Player B has A-A-Q-7-2. Player A wins because the king kicker beats the queen kicker. Understanding kicker strength is essential — many players lose significant chips by misjudging how often their kicker is dominated.

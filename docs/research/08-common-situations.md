# 08 — Common Situations and Concepts

---

## Three-Betting

A three-bet is a re-raise preflop (or the third bet on any street). The original raiser made the first raise; the three-bettor raises again. Three-bets dramatically increase the pot size and build strong range advantages for the three-bettor.

Three-bets are made for value (with strong hands that benefit from a larger pot) or as bluffs (to gain fold equity against the original raiser's somewhat wide range). Balanced three-bet ranges include both.

---

## Squeeze

A squeeze is a three-bet made when one player has raised and at least one other player has flat-called. The presence of callers increases fold equity for the squeezer because:

1. The original raiser must now beat not just the squeezer, but also the caller(s) who may wake up with strong hands
2. The callers likely have wide, speculative ranges (they would have three-bet with strong holdings) — they are prime fold targets

---

## Donk Betting

A donk bet is a bet made by the player who is out of position into the player who was the last preflop aggressor. It is called a donk bet because historically it was considered a weak or uninformed play — you are "leading out" into a player with a perceived strong range before that player has had the chance to c-bet.

In modern theory, donk bets have legitimate strategic uses:

- Protecting a range of medium-strength hands against a certain board type
- Representing boards that the out-of-position player's range hits well
- Disrupting the in-position player's ability to realize their equity cheaply

---

## Check-Raising

A check-raise is the act of first checking, then raising after an opponent bets. It is a powerful play because:

1. It shows extreme strength (or a sophisticated bluff)
2. It builds the pot from out of position
3. It denies opponents the opportunity to see the next card cheaply

Check-raising with strong hands disguises their strength — opponents who would have called a lead bet are now facing a raise and may re-evaluate their own holdings.

Check-raises can also be bluffs or semi-bluffs (check-raising with a strong draw that has equity even if called).

---

## Slow Playing

Slow playing means checking or calling with a very strong hand to disguise its strength and encourage opponents to build the pot before applying pressure. Example: flopping a full house and checking to induce a bluff from an aggressive opponent.

Slow playing is profitable when:

- The opponent will not improve to a hand strong enough to beat you
- The opponent will bet if given the opportunity
- The opponent's range is unlikely to contain hands that would give action anyway (so betting for value has lower EV than inducing a bluff)

Slow playing is dangerous when:

- Free cards can improve opponent hands to beat you (never slow play against obvious draws)
- Opponents are passive and will not bet if given the chance (checking denies value)

---

## The Free Card Play

In position, a player with a draw can sometimes raise on an early street to take a "free card" on the next street. Example: raising a flop c-bet with a flush draw. The opponent, fearing another bet, checks the turn. Now the player can check back and see the river for free (or cheaply). This play gains equity realization at reduced cost.

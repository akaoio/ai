# 04 — Position

Position is arguably the single most important structural concept in Texas Hold'em. It determines the order in which players act during a betting round, and acting last — having **position** on your opponents — is one of the most durable and exploitable edges in the game.

---

## Why Position Matters

When you act last, you have seen every other player's action before making your decision. You know whether they checked (signaling potential weakness), bet (signaling potential strength or a bluff attempt), and for how much. This information is permanently unavailable to players who acted before you.

When you act first (out of position), you must make decisions in the dark. You do not know whether your opponents will fold, call, or raise after you act. This forces you to use more defensive, conservative strategies because you cannot react to information you do not yet have.

Position is a permanent advantage on every postflop street for the entire duration of a hand. It does not change between the flop, turn, and river. If you have position on the flop, you have it on the turn and river too.

---

## Seat Names and Their Order

In a 6-player game, positions from earliest to latest to act postflop are:

1. **Small Blind (SB)** — worst position postflop; acts first on every street
2. **Big Blind (BB)** — acts second postflop; has the positional disadvantage but has already invested one BB
3. **Under the Gun (UTG)** — first to act preflop; third postflop
4. **Middle Position (MP)** — moderate position
5. **Cutoff (CO)** — one seat to the right of the button; strong position
6. **Button (BTN)** — acts last on every postflop street; the strongest position

In a 9-player game, additional positions fill between UTG and the Cutoff (UTG+1, UTG+2, Lojack, Hijack).

---

## The Button

The button is the most coveted seat because it guarantees last action postflop for the entire hand. Button players can:

- See the entire round of action before deciding
- Bet to represent strength when opponents check
- Control pot size more effectively
- Profitably play a wider range of starting hands
- Bluff more freely with lower risk

A significant portion of an experienced player's overall profit comes from hands played on the button.

---

## Positional Hand Selection

Position directly informs which hands are worth playing. A hand that is marginally profitable on the button may be a clear fold from early position because:

- In early position, many players yet to act can have a stronger hand
- There are more opportunities to be raised out of the pot
- Postflop, you will be forced to act without information on most streets

This is why experienced players **open raise a wide range from the button** and a much narrower range from early position. This is not arbitrary — it reflects the mathematical reality of positional advantage compounded over many betting rounds.

---

## Playing Out of Position

When forced to play out of position (e.g., from the blinds after defending a raise), several adjustments apply:

- Check more often to deny opponents free information about hand strength
- Use check-raises to recapture initiative
- Keep pots smaller with marginal hands to limit the damage of acting without information
- Be more cautious with medium-strength hands that do not improve dramatically
